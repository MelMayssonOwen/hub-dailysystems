What are the best YNAB alternatives in 2026?

● Updated August 2026  |  sources cited inline

Short answer: The closest YNAB alternatives are PocketGuard ($12.99/mo or $74.99/yr) for a simpler spendable-cash view, Monarch for couples and net-worth tracking, Rocket Money if your main goal is cutting subscriptions, and a spreadsheet (Vertex42 free, or Tiller $99/yr with bank sync) if you want YNAB's zero-based method without the subscription. YNAB itself is $14.99/mo or $109/yr with a 34-day free trial. Pick based on whether you want YNAB's strict method, a lighter app, or full spreadsheet control.

People leave YNAB for three reasons: the price, the learning curve of zero-based budgeting, or a wish to cancel subscriptions rather than budget. Each points to a different alternative.

Can a spreadsheet replace YNAB?

Yes, if you are willing to run the method by hand. YNAB's core idea — zero-based budgeting, where every dollar is assigned a job before you spend it — is fully reproducible in a spreadsheet. Free Vertex42 templates or a paid template give you the grid; Tiller adds the automatic bank sync that YNAB has built in. What you lose is YNAB's guided workflow and reminders. We explain the method itself in what is zero-based budgeting.

One option among many If your real reason for leaving YNAB is subscription creep rather than budgeting style, a one-off audit like SpendCull (free scan, $99 report, no bank login) targets that directly — though it does not budget. Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest YNAB alternative?

A free Vertex42 spreadsheet is the cheapest way to run YNAB's zero-based method without paying. Among apps, PocketGuard ($12.99/mo or $74.99/yr) undercuts YNAB ($14.99/mo or $109/yr), and Rocket Money has a free tier.

Can a spreadsheet do everything YNAB does?

Largely, yes. YNAB's zero-based method — assigning every dollar a job — works in any spreadsheet. Free Vertex42 templates give you the grid; Tiller ($99/yr) adds the automatic bank sync YNAB has built in. You lose YNAB's guided workflow and reminders.

Which YNAB alternative is best for couples?

Monarch is commonly chosen for couples and net-worth tracking. A shared spreadsheet (Vertex42 or Tiller) also works well for two people who both maintain it.

Is YNAB worth the price?

YNAB is worth $109/yr for people who want a strict, guided zero-based system and will use it consistently — its users are among the most devoted in personal finance. If you want something lighter or cheaper, PocketGuard or a spreadsheet is a better fit.
