Money, budgeting & habit FAQ

● Updated August 2026  |  sources cited inline

Straight answers to the questions people ask most about budgeting apps, spreadsheets, subscriptions, and habit tracking. Each links to a fuller guide with sources.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to build a habit?

A median of 66 days to reach automaticity per the UCL study (Lally et al., 2010), ranging from 18 to 254 days. The '21 days' claim is a myth. See our guide on building a habit that sticks.

Do habit tracking apps actually work?

They work when you track 1-3 habits and logging takes seconds. The tracker's features matter far less than whether you keep using it past the roughly two-month automaticity mark.

Are paid budgeting apps worth it over free ones?

They're worth it when the fee is smaller than the money saved and when automatic sync removes the friction that makes people quit manual tracking. Otherwise a free spreadsheet does the same math.

Is it safe to link my bank to a budgeting app?

For reputable apps, yes: they use read-only aggregators that can't move money, and the CFPB's Section 1033 rule lets you revoke access anytime. Prefer read-only apps and audit connections periodically.

How do I cancel subscriptions I forgot about?

Export three months of statements, sort by merchant to surface recurring charges, and cancel each at the source. It's free and takes about 30 minutes; tools can automate finding them.

What's the easiest budgeting method?

50/30/20 — split after-tax income into 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings and debt. It's the best default for beginners; zero-based offers more control for more effort.

Is a spreadsheet better than a budgeting app?

A spreadsheet wins on control and cost; an app wins on automation and reminders. Choose a spreadsheet if you'll do weekly manual entry, an app if you won't.

How do I stop overspending in the moment?

Add friction: a 24-hour waiting rule, delete saved cards, unsubscribe from sale emails, and use if-then plans. Research on the 'one sec' nudge (PNAS 2023) found a pause cut tempting-app opens by 57%.

Which is cheaper, YNAB or PocketGuard?

PocketGuard ($12.99/mo or $74.99/yr) undercuts YNAB ($14.99/mo or $109/yr). A free Vertex42 spreadsheet is cheaper than both.

Is Tiller worth $99 a year?

Only for automatic bank sync. If you'll enter transactions manually, free Vertex42 does the same budgeting for nothing; Tiller's 30-day trial lets you test the automation.
