How do you cancel subscriptions you forgot about?

● Updated August 2026  |  sources cited inline

Direct answer: Export three months of bank and card statements, sort by merchant to surface every recurring charge, flag anything you did not knowingly use in the last month, and cancel each one at the source (the vendor's account page, your app store, or your card issuer). This takes about 30 minutes and costs nothing. The steps below are the exact process, no tool required, though a tool can automate the "find them all" part.

The checklist

  1. Export 3 months of statements. Download CSV or PDF from each bank and card. Three months catches monthly, and you will spot annual renewals by scanning back further for the same merchant.
  2. Sort by merchant / description. In a spreadsheet, sort the description column so identical merchants group together. Recurring charges line up as repeats at the same amount.
  3. Mark every recurring line. Streaming, apps, cloud storage, gym, news, software, memberships, "premium" upgrades, and anything with a round monthly price.
  4. Score each: used in the last month? If no, it is a cancel candidate. If you are not sure, that uncertainty is itself a signal you are not getting value.
  5. Cancel at the source. App Store / Google Play subscriptions cancel in your store account; web subscriptions in the vendor's billing settings. Screenshot the confirmation.
  6. Block re-bills you can't cancel. If a vendor makes cancelling hard, contact your card issuer to stop the merchant or issue a new card number.
  7. Re-check in 30 days. Free trials convert and "paused" plans restart. Put a recurring calendar reminder to repeat this scan quarterly.
One option among many Step 1, 4 (finding them all) is the tedious part. SpendCull, from this site's publisher, automates exactly that: upload the CSV export, get a prioritised cut list with dollar estimates, no bank login (free scan, $99 full report). It does not cancel anything, so you still do steps 5, 7 yourself. Rocket Money automates the cancelling too if you prefer that; compare on SpendCull vs Rocket Money. The manual method above is completely free.

Why this works

Recurring charges exploit inattention: once set up, they require an active decision to stop, and most people never revisit them. Making the charges visible in one list converts a vague "I should cancel some stuff" into concrete line items you can act on, the same reason self-monitoring changes behaviour in the budgeting research. The CFPB's consumer guidance recommends reviewing statements regularly for exactly this reason.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find all my subscriptions?

Export three months of bank and card statements, sort by merchant so recurring charges group together, and flag every repeating line at the same amount. That surfaces monthly subscriptions; scan back further to catch annual renewals. A CSV-based audit tool can automate this step.

Can I cancel subscriptions without an app?

Yes. The entire process is free and manual: export statements, list recurring charges, cancel each at the vendor's billing page or your app store, and block any stubborn re-bills through your card issuer. No paid tool is required.

How often should I check for forgotten subscriptions?

Quarterly. Free trials convert to paid plans, paused subscriptions restart, and new ones accumulate. A recurring calendar reminder every three months keeps recurring spend from creeping back up.

What is a zombie subscription?

A zombie subscription is a recurring charge you keep paying but no longer use, such as a streaming service, app, or membership you forgot about. They persist because stopping them requires an active decision most people never make.
