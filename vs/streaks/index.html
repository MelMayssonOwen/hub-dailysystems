Patterns vs Streaks: which habit tracker fits how you actually behave?

● Updated August 2026  |  sources cited inline

Short answer: Choose Streaks if you are all-Apple, want a one-time purchase with no subscription, and like a strict daily streak that pushes you not to miss. Choose Patterns if you tend to over-commit and quit: it caps you at 1, 3 habits and uses "resilience streaks" that survive one missed day, matching the research on realistic habit formation ($9.99/mo or $49.99/yr, 2-week trial). Streaks is cheaper over time; Patterns is more forgiving by design. Both are iOS; only Streaks also covers Watch, Mac and Vision Pro.

Strict streak or forgiving streak, which builds the habit?

The research favours forgiveness. Reaching automaticity takes a median of 66 days (Lally et al., 2010), and along the way an occasional miss does not meaningfully derail progress, Lally's data showed a single lapse did not reset the trajectory. James Clear turned that into the rule "never miss twice." A strict tracker that zeroes your streak after one slip can trigger the "I broke it, why bother" spiral. Streaks' strict model motivates some people; Patterns' resilience model is built specifically to avoid that failure mode. Neither is universally right.

Disclosure Patterns is made by Opsibyte, the publisher of this site. Straight talk: Streaks is a one-time purchase, so it is cheaper than Patterns' $49.99/yr, and it works across more Apple devices. Patterns only makes sense if its opinionated constraints, very few habits, forgiving streaks, match your failure pattern. If they do not, Streaks (or free Loop/Habitica) is the better buy.

Frequently asked questions

Is Patterns or Streaks better for building habits?

It depends on your failure pattern. Streaks' strict daily streak motivates people who respond to not breaking a chain. Patterns' resilience streak survives one miss, matching James Clear's 'never miss twice' rule and Lally et al.'s finding that a single lapse doesn't reset progress, better for people who quit after one slip.

Which is cheaper, Patterns or Streaks?

Streaks is a one-time App Store purchase with no subscription, so over time it is cheaper than Patterns at $9.99/mo or $49.99/yr. Patterns offers a 2-week trial to test it first.

Do Patterns and Streaks work on Android?

No. Both are Apple-only. Streaks covers iPhone, Apple Watch, iPad, Mac and Vision Pro; Patterns is iOS/iPadOS. Android users should use Habitify or the free Loop Habit Tracker instead.

How many habits should I track?

The research suggests 1-3. Tracking too many at once is a common reason people abandon habit apps. Patterns enforces this cap; with Streaks you can add up to 24 tasks but the same advice applies.
